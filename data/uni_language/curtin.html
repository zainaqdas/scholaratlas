Ways to meet English language requirements

Before you start your degree, you must be competent in reading, writing and speaking English. There are many qualifications and tests you can use to demonstrate your English competency. Here are the most common ways to meet our minimum English language requirements.

  • Undergraduate degrees
  • Postgraduate degrees
  • ATAR English/English Literature/English as an Additional Language or Dialect, scaled score of at least 50
  • English General Grade A (Grade B also accepted for portfolio entry only)
  • STAT Written English, score of at least 140
  • Successful completion of UniReady units: Fundamentals of Academic Writing and Foundations of Communication, or equivalent
  • Successful completion of our UniReady or Indigenous enabling program

  • STAT Written English, score of at least 140

Didn’t get the year 12 English score you wanted?

Sitting STAT WE is a popular way to meet English competency if you didn’t achieve the score in English you needed in your General or ATAR studies in year 12. You can sit the Written English (WE) section of the Skills for Tertiary Admissions Test (STAT), and if you achieve a score of at least 140 on the test, you’ll meet our minimum English language requirement.

Your questions, answered

If you’ve applied for a course through TISC and didn’t achieve a scaled score of 50 or more in ATAR English/Literature/EALD, then a combined course score, a moderated school assessment, or exam mark of at least 55 or a letter grade of A, B or C in your English ATAR subject will meet Curtin’s English requirement.

TISC will assess this during the first offer round process. If you still do not meet our English requirements, TISC will invite you to sit the Written English (WE) section of the Skills for Tertiary Admissions Test (STAT) over the summer. If you achieve a score of at least 140 on the WE test, you’ll have met the required level of English and you will be considered for your preferred course in TISC’s second round of offers.

If you’ve applied for a course directly through Curtin, Curtin will notify you if you do not meet the English requirements; you will need to book to sit the STAT WE through TISC.

If you haven’t achieved a grade A in General English (or a grade B in General English for portfolio entry), you can sit the Written English (WE) section of the Skills for Tertiary Admissions Test (STAT). If you achieve a score of at least 140 WE on the STAT, you’ll have met the required level of English.

If you’ve applied for a course through TISC, TISC will invite you to sit the STAT WE over the summer. If you have applied for a course directly through Curtin, you will need to book to sit the STAT WE through TISC.

STAT stands for the Skills for Special Tertiary Admissions Test (STAT). It’s designed to assess your suitability for learning at university level. The test comprises two sections: Multiple Choice (MC) and Written English (WE).

STAT WE is a one-hour test that involves writing two short essays. If you achieve a score of 140 or higher on the STAT WE, you’ll have met Curtin’s required level of English.

If you’re currently in high school and your ATAR English score is below 50, TISC will automatically invite you to sit the STAT WE before the second round of offers. If you achieve a minimum score of 140 on the test, you will be considered for your preferred course in the second round of offers.

If you are mature age (you will be at least 20 years of age the year you want to start university), you can sit both sections of the STAT (MC and WE) to meet Curtin’s entry requirements, including English.

Visit the TISC website to learn more about STAT.

Our undergraduate certificates are ‘open entry’, which means there are no specific entry requirements. This means you do not need to meet any English language requirements to apply for these courses.

For our UniReady Enabling Program, it’s recommended that you have a basic level of English literacy, but there is no specific English language requirement you must meet.

Domestic students

You are considered a domestic student if you are:

  • a citizen of Australia or New Zealand
  • a permanent resident of Australia
  • an Australian Permanent Humanitarian visa holder

If this doesn’t sound like you, or if content for international students is more relevant, you can switch at any time.

International students

You are considered an International student if you are:

  • a citizen of any country except Australia or New Zealand
  • a temporary resident of Australia
  • a permanent resident of New Zealand

If this doesn’t sound like you, or if content for domestic students is more relevant, you can switch at any time.
